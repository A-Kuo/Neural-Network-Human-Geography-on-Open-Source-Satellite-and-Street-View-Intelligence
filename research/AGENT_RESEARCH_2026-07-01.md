## Summary
Cross-view geospatial ML is consolidating around two patterns directly relevant to this repo: (1) large pretrained location/scene embedding foundation models (AlphaEarth, SatCLIP, GeoCLIP, UniGeoCLIP) that replace hand-built CNN feature extractors with a shared embedding space across satellite, street-view, coordinate, and text modalities, and (2) contrastive cross-view retrieval/localization architectures purpose-built for matching satellite tiles to street-level panoramas. Both point toward the same near-term upgrade path for this repo: swap or augment any from-scratch CNN scene encoder with a pretrained contrastive embedding backbone, and adopt a proper cross-view retrieval evaluation protocol (Recall@K on a held-out geo-split) instead of ad hoc accuracy metrics.

## Engineering Insights
- Quantize embeddings for storage: AlphaEarth-style deployments cut 32-bit float embeddings to 8-bit with negligible accuracy loss — a 4x storage reduction that matters once this repo starts caching precomputed satellite/street embeddings at scale.
- Design for missing modalities: UrbanFusion's stochastic modality dropout during training (randomly zeroing street-view or satellite branches) makes the fused model robust to inference-time gaps (e.g., no street-view coverage in rural tiles) — cheap to add to any dual-encoder setup.
- Use a proper geo-split for evaluation: cross-view geo-localization papers uniformly evaluate with Recall@1/5/10 on spatially held-out test regions (not random image splits), since random splits leak nearby-tile correlation and inflate reported accuracy.
- Prefer pretrained location/scene encoders over from-scratch CNNs when labeled data is limited: SatCLIP and AlphaEarth are explicitly positioned as few-shot-friendly embedding sources — freeze-and-probe (train only a small head) is the recommended first move before any fine-tuning.
- Watch the "decentrality" failure mode: real street-view images are rarely centered under their matching satellite tile; benchmarks that assume centered alignment (like early CVUSA-style setups) overstate real-world performance.
